# Review R3: MARL practice, reproducible RL evaluation, deployment

Manuscript: "An Execution-Time CBF-QP Safety Filter on Frozen MAPPO Formation Policies: A Pre-Specified Paired Evaluation in Simulation" (target IEEE RA-L / L-CSS). Line numbers refer to `paper/main.tex`. Supporting files read: `docs/MAPPO_POLICY.md`, `config/study.toml`, `results/phase1/checkpoint_selection.json`, `audit/PROVENANCE.md`, plus `results/curriculum/seed_*/stages.json`, `results/gate_v2/GATE.md`, `results/final_test/FINAL_TEST.md`, `results/final_test/paired_timeout_stalled.csv`, `scripts/mechanism_exploratory.py`, and both protocol documents in `docs/`. I did not modify any file or run any simulation.

## 1. Summary and recommendation

The paper tests one decentralised sampled-data CBF-QP filter on nine frozen MAPPO actors: three training seeds, with three final-stage checkpoints each. The test uses 9,000 paired layouts per density at K = 1, 2, 3, under criteria locked before the test. It reports a clean negative verdict: criterion (a), fewer failures, was met; criteria (b) and (c), non-inferiority in GOAL and formation, were not. On the RL-evaluation side the work is unusually careful. It has a locked protocol, reserved test layouts, and checkpoints chosen by an iteration rule rather than by performance. It keeps every trial, reports per-seed and per-actor results, keeps exploratory analyses separate, and lists its deviations in full. The confirmatory statistics hold up.

The problems are in interpretation and in describing the policy population. Several facts are reported as history without their consequences:
- The actors failed the study's own competence gate.
- The primary densities were picked after filter-off results for these actors had been seen.
- The final training stage used K = 2, 5, 8, so K = 1 and K = 3 lie outside it. K = 5 and 8 make up two thirds of that stage, and the actors almost never solve them.
- The "validation" layouts also controlled when the curriculum advanced to the next stage.
- At K = 1 the pooled shortfall comes almost entirely from one of the three training seeds.

Other problems:
- The non-inferiority margins are given without any rationale. The 1.2 cm formation "failure" at K = 1 has unclear practical meaning.
- The pre-declared stalled flag marks nearly every TIMEOUT in both arms, including filter off, so it cannot separate deadlock from slow progress. The H3 discussion relies on it.
- Two practical implications are weakly supported: a trial-level, partly mechanical AUC, and "clearance-aware training" when training already penalised clearance below 15 cm. The implication best supported by the data (an infeasibility fallback and deadlock recovery) is missing.
- Several terms are used before they are defined or are defined inconsistently. Examples: the stall classes, "near-goal parking stall", "held-out", "first configuration", and "0.05/3".

**Recommendation: major revision.** The confirmatory core is sound and should be kept as it is. The interpretation needs to state what the policy population is: a weak, collision-prone family, assessed partly outside its training distribution, with 3 training runs. It also needs to state how that limits the "not shown useful" verdict and the practical implications. None of the fixes needs new trials.

# Reviewer 2 report (CBFs, sampled-data safety, multi-robot control, safe MARL)

Manuscript: "An Execution-Time CBF-QP Safety Filter on Frozen MAPPO Formation Policies: A Pre-Specified Paired Evaluation in Simulation"

## 1. Summary and recommendation

The paper places a decentralised, sampled-data CBF-QP filter (offset-point barriers, cover disc, equal-split pair rows, state-only second-order margin) between nine frozen MAPPO actors and a three-robot unicycle team. It runs a pre-registered paired evaluation on 9,000 reserved layouts per density. Failures (COLLISION-or-INFEASIBLE) fell at every density, but non-inferiority in GOAL and formation error failed, so the filter was "not shown useful". Exploratory analyses then attribute the cost to stalls in front of obstacles and the 433 INFEASIBLE trials to "numerical sensitivity at αΔ = 1".

What I checked. I re-derived Lemma 1 and the margins in eq. (4). Both are correct: |p̈| = |ω||ṗ| ≤ ω̄V_p; ḧ_o = 2|ṗ|² + 2(p−c)·p̈; |p(t)−c| ≤ d + V_pΔ; the pair version uses relative speed ≤ 2V_p and relative acceleration ≤ 2A_p; δ = MΔ/2; and h(t_k+s) ≥ h_0(1−αs) + (Ms/2)(Δ−s). Plugging in numbers gives δ_o(D_o)/α = 0.004536 m² at α = 10, which matches results/design/margin_constants.json. The cover-disc argument and the code (cbf_qp.py, bmatch.py, protocol.py) match the text.

The main problems are with interpretation and definitions, not with the derivation:
- The paper's central mechanistic claim, that infeasibility comes from numerical sensitivity at the lemma boundary αΔ = 1, is not supported by its own data:
  - the binding states sit at the stand-off h* > 0, not near h = 0;
  - the zero-command condition h ≥ δ/α holds at every gain;
  - the validation sweep has INFEASIBLE outcomes at every gain, and most of them at α = 0.5.
- The analytic guarantee is never stated as a proposition, and the paper calls it "numerical". Its assumptions (A1–A9) are cited but never listed.
- The equal-split hypothesis is not stated fully.
- Several quantities are used before they are defined: the stand-off, the zero-command slack, the near-boundary diagnostic, intervention, and the closing-speed cap.
- The cover-disc geometry predicts a heading-dependent required body clearance of about 0.6–10.6 cm at α = 10. This is the obvious analytic explanation for the observed group-A clearances (median 3.3 cm), but the paper does not connect the two.

**Recommendation: Major revision.** The experimental design, pre-registration and disclosures are unusually careful, and the confirmatory result is credible. However, the paper's statement of what the filter guarantees and its explanation of infeasibility must be corrected before publication. All fixes are textual or small re-analyses of saved logs; no new trials are needed.

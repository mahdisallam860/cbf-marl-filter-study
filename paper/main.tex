\begin{abstract}
We evaluate whether an execution-time, decentralised control-barrier-function quadratic-programme
(CBF-QP) safety filter, placed between frozen multi-agent PPO (MAPPO) formation policies and a
simulated differential-drive team, is useful under criteria fixed before the test. Nine frozen
actors (three training seeds, three checkpoints each) were evaluated on reserved, previously
unused layouts with one, two and three obstacles (\PairsPerDensity{} paired trials per density),
with the filter off, the filter on at the gain $\alpha^\star=\AlphaStar$ selected on validation
layouts, and a brake comparator. The filter reduced the share of trials ending in collision or
infeasibility at every density (from \FailOffone\% to \FailOnone\%, \FailOfftwo\% to \FailOntwo\% and
\FailOffthree\% to \FailOnthree\%). Under the pre-set criteria it was nevertheless \emph{not shown
useful} at any density: the non-inferiority criteria for goal completion (margin five percentage
points) and for formation error over the common executed prefix (margin 0.01\,m) were not met.
Exploratory, post hoc analyses of the saved logs indicate that many filtered runs stalled in
front of obstacles on layouts the unfiltered policy completed or collided on, and that all
\InfTotal{} infeasible quadratic programmes sat at the barrier stand-off, consistent with
numerical sensitivity at $\alpha\Delta=1$. Claims cover these nine actors in this simulated
scenario only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Reinforcement-learning policies for multi-robot formations are commonly trained without safety
constraints, and a safety filter can be added at execution time without retraining. Whether such a
filter is \emph{useful}, i.e. reduces failures without an unacceptable loss of task completion or
formation quality, is an empirical question for a given policy family. This draft reports a
pre-specified paired evaluation of one such filter, a decentralised sampled-data CBF-QP, on frozen
MAPPO actors in a VMAS differential-drive formation-navigation scenario, followed by clearly
labelled exploratory analyses of why the pre-set criteria were not met.

\section{Related work}
Execution-time filtering of policies trained without the filter appears in
\cite{vinod2022} (centralised QP, linear and double-integrator agents); filtering during training
and execution with model-predictive shields for unicycle formations appears in \cite{dawood2023}.
Sampled-data CBF margins that account for inter-sample behaviour are studied in
\cite{singletary2020,breeden2022,tan_zocbf,liu_sacbf}; these margins are input-independent bounds over the input
set and do not depend on the applied command. Decentralised equal-split ("half-responsibility") constraints go back to
\cite{wang2017,vandenberg2011}, and stopping as a feasibility-preserving backup is used in
\cite{chen2021}. The offset (barrier) point for unicycles is used in \cite{emam2019}. Hidden
assumptions in CBF safety claims are discussed in \cite{kim2026}. Input-dependent inter-sample
bounds used for trigger timing appear in \cite{yang2019}.

\section{System and filter}
Three differential-drive robots (radius 0.10\,m, $v\in[-0.5,0.5]$\,m/s, $|\omega|\le 1$\,rad/s)
move a triangular formation (slot radius 0.30\,m) through a field of circular obstacles (radius
0.20\,m) to a goal; control period $\Delta=0.1$\,s, horizon 400 periods. GOAL requires the centroid
within 0.10\,m of the goal and mean slot error within 0.10\,m; COLLISION is scored on the swept
path and ends the trial; an infeasible QP ends the trial as INFEASIBLE before any motion; otherwise
TIMEOUT. The filter (``filter C'') constrains a point offset $\ell=0.05$\,m ahead of each axle with
barriers $h=\|p-c\|^2-(R+\ell+R_o)^2$ (obstacles) and $h=\|p_i-p_j\|^2-(2(R+\ell))^2$ (pairs, equal
split), a sampled-data margin from a two-sided bound on $\ddot h$ (design document, Lemma~1), and
solves one two-variable QP per robot exactly. The B-match comparator applies $(0,\omega_{\rm nom})$
whenever the clipped nominal command violates one of the filter's rows at the same gain; it is not
a certified stop (the offset point rotates).

\section{Experimental design}
The protocol was locked by the author before any filter-on data existed (the margins and decisions
were chosen without advisor review; see Section~\ref{sec:disclosures}). Nine actors were fixed by
a checkpoint rule (early/middle/late of the final curriculum stage, seeds 0--2). The gain was
selected on existing validation layouts from $\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2,5,10\}$\,s$^{-1}$ by the highest
pooled filter-on GOAL count over the nine actors and $K=1,2,3$. The final test used 1{,}000
reserved layouts per actor and density, each run with the filter off, the filter on at
$\alpha^\star$ and B-match at $\alpha^\star$. Usefulness at a density required all of: (a) a
one-sided exact McNemar reduction in COLLISION-or-INFEASIBLE; (b) GOAL loss (off minus on) with
one-sided 98.33\% upper bound (Newcombe method 10) at most 0.05; (c) the equal-actor-weighted mean
increase in formation error over the common executed prefix with stratified bootstrap 98.33\%
upper bound at most 0.01\,m; each one-sided at $0.05/3$.

Table~\ref{tab:conf} gives the confirmatory results. Criterion (a) was met at every density;
(b) and (c) were met at none, so the filter was \emph{not shown useful under the pre-set
criteria} at $K=1,2,3$. With the filter on there were \CollOnone, \CollOntwo{} and
\CollOnthree{} collisions and \InfOnone, \InfOntwo{} and \InfOnthree{} INFEASIBLE trials;
TIMEOUT rose from \TOoffone\%, \TOofftwo\% and \TOoffthree\% to \TOonone\%, \TOontwo\% and
\TOonthree\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:f2}). B-match had \CollBmone, \CollBmtwo{} and \CollBmthree{} collisions
and \InfBmone, \InfBmtwo{} and \InfBmthree{} INFEASIBLE outcomes, and reached GOAL in \GoalBmone\%,

\OnMinusBmGoaltwo{} and \OnMinusBmGoalthree{} percentage points more often (descriptive).
Per-actor GOAL losses vary widely (Fig.~\ref{fig:f3}); a pooled result does not establish
usefulness, or its absence, for each actor.

\section{Exploratory mechanism analysis (exploratory, post hoc)}
\emph{Everything in this section is exploratory and post hoc: categories, thresholds and groups were
chosen after the confirmatory results were known, the analyses read only saved logs, and they
support no causal or confirmatory claim.}

\paragraph{Stall classes.} Of \TOonTotal{} filter-on TIMEOUTs, \TOfi{} (\TOfiPct\%) were classified
as filter-induced stalls (the clipped nominal command violated an enabled barrier row in at least
half of the last 50 periods), \TOfiObst{} of them involving obstacle rows; on the same layouts the
unfiltered run collided in \TOfiOffColl{} and reached GOAL in \TOfiOffGoal{} cases. Another \TOng{}
(\TOngPct\%) were near-goal parking stalls, \TOngOffTO{} of them on layouts where the unfiltered run
also timed out.

\paragraph{Groups A and B.} Group A (\GroupA{} pairs: unfiltered GOAL, filtered filter-induced
stall; \GroupAobst{} obstacle-labelled, \GroupAnb{} neighbour-only) and group B (\GroupB{} pairs:
both GOAL) were selected by observed outcome. The unfiltered runs of group A came closer to
obstacles (median sampled minimum surface clearance \GapAmedCm\,cm, group A only) than those of
group B (\GapBmedCm\,cm; Fig.~\ref{fig:f4}). Their applied commands violated at least one filter
obstacle row in \ACobstViolPct\% (A) versus \BCobstViolPct\% (B) of runs, and a robot-pair row in
\ACrrViolPct\% versus \BCrrViolPct\%.

\paragraph{Clearance thresholds.} Of all trials, \ThrOffGoal\% reached GOAL unfiltered, and
\ThrMatchObstFive\% did so while keeping a sampled minimum obstacle clearance of at least 5\,cm,
against a filter-on GOAL rate of \ThrOnGoal\% on the same layouts (Fig.~\ref{fig:f5}). This
describes how close the unfiltered runs came to obstacles; it does not measure what a buffer or
any filter would cost. Among unfiltered GOAL trials the descriptive AUC of the unfiltered minimum
obstacle clearance for ``filtered run did not reach GOAL'' was \AucPooled.

\paragraph{Infeasibility.} All \InfTotal{} INFEASIBLE trials were reproduced (\InfRepro{} of
\InfTotal) by the unchanged filter code at the saved states. In every case the binding row's
barrier value was within \InfHdiffMax{}\,m$^2$ of its stand-off $h^\star=\delta/\alpha$ and the zero
command violated it by between \InfSlackMax{} and \InfSlackMin{} (normalised); \InfNear{} of
\InfRobots{} met a pre-declared near-boundary diagnostic. This is consistent with numerical
sensitivity near the boundary, not proof of the cause.

\paragraph{Transit.} During formation transit the equal-split cap on a robot's closing speed toward
a neighbour had median \CapMedMin--\CapMedMax\,m/s, above the largest barrier-point speed
\Vp\,m/s, and bound in \CapBindMin--\CapBindMax\% of moving closing periods.

\section{Discussion}
\emph{Collisions versus stalls.} The filter removed almost all collisions (Table~\ref{tab:conf});
in the exploratory classification most filtered timeouts were stalls with active obstacle rows, mostly
on layouts where the unfiltered run collided or reached GOAL (stall classes above). We read this as a
description of where the lost completions occurred, not as a causal attribution.

\emph{Small clearances.} The completions lost in group A involved unfiltered runs that passed
close to obstacles (median \GapAmedCm\,cm, group A only; Fig.~\ref{fig:f4}).

\emph{Comparator.} The filter reached GOAL \OnBmGoalMin--\OnBmGoalMax{} percentage points more often
than B-match on the same layouts, with more INFEASIBLE outcomes (results above).

\emph{Gain at the lemma boundary.} The selected gain gives $\alpha^\star\Delta=1$. At
alpha*dt = 1, the lemma provides no strictly positive endpoint lower bound from the initial
barrier alone. Numerical robustness requires separate analysis. The infeasibility audit found the
recorded infeasible states consistent with numerical sensitivity near this boundary (infeasibility
paragraph).

\emph{Predictions.} A methodology report written before the validation sweep, but committed to the
repository only after the final test (\texttt{docs/methodology/README.md}), predicted that the
selected gain would lie in $[5,10]$ and that small gains would conflict with holding the formation;
the sweep selected $\alpha^\star=\AlphaStar$ and gave \SelGoalhalf{} filter-on GOAL outcomes with
\SelInfhalf{} INFEASIBLE at $\alpha=0.5$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:f1}). Because of the commit order these are
documented, not pre-registered, predictions.

\emph{Remark (ZR).} For these offset-point barriers the second-order sampled-data margin can be
evaluated at the held command and written as two affine rows per barrier, which makes the zero
command admissible whenever $h\ge 0$ in exact arithmetic (design document, Section~4a). Its
ingredients are existing ideas \cite{breeden2022,vandenberg2011,chen2021}, the inclusion of
admissible sets is non-strict, and the property does not survive float32 rounding without a
separate tolerance rule; it was not implemented or evaluated here.

\section{Limitations}
The study uses one simulated scenario, nine fixed actors from one custom MAPPO implementation,
three obstacle densities and one gain. Validation layouts were reused for gain tuning. The
formation criterion covers the common executed prefix only. Safety statements are numerical under
the design-document assumptions, not a continuous-time proof. The exploratory analyses are
selected by observed outcome and descriptive.

\item Claims cover these nine fixed actors in this simulated scenario only.
\item Safety statements are numerical under the design-document assumptions (A1--A9), not a continuous-time proof.
\item The MAPPO implementation is custom and was sanity-checked only on the stock VMAS navigation task.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
Under the pre-set criteria, the execution-time CBF-QP filter at $\alpha^\star=\AlphaStar$ was not
shown useful for these nine frozen MAPPO actors at any of the three densities: it reduced
collision-or-infeasible failures but did not meet the non-inferiority criteria for goal completion
and common-prefix formation error.
